The plotted velocity estimate shows an overall velocity curve that is slightly obscured by measurment noise.
The noise is especially visible at the extrema of the velocity estimate.
The PID controller used for our system uses the velocity estimate to compute the derviatve of the error. The added noise measurement noise can introduce instability, if the D-gain is too high. 
As an effect, the maximum D-Gain is limited by the noise of the velocity estimate.

Postlab Q2:

Increasing the window size has two effects. First, increasing the filter size leads to a lower effective cutoff frequency. High frequency components increasingly get reduced, the more they are above this frequency.
However, in the provided trajectory the high frequency noise is already mostly filtered out by the n=5 filter and the smoothness of the curves increases only marginally. Consequentially, only the low-frequency base signal amplitude gets reduced for higher averaging numbers. 

Second, the window size determines the phase shift of the fitlered signal compared to the raw signal. With increasing window size the phase shift increases as well. 
A filter with a window size of n=20 seems to introduce around 0.16 s of phase shift, but since the n=5 filter already eliminates the majority of the noise, it was selected as the highest performance filter window size.
